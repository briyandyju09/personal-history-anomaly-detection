1 Motivation

A personalised detector scores each transaction against one person's own history. When an account opens, that history is empty.

  • Deployed systems filter out “unseasoned users”
  • The cutoff is assumed, not measured
  • We found no published measurement of it
  • Thin histories: new customers, young people, recent immigrants
Q: Where does the personal baseline become worth using?

10 A second dataset reverses a ranking
TabFormer (synthetic). Same shape, later threshold; isolation forest is best at n = 5 to 50.
  • Mahalanobis threshold is 250, not 25
  • Isolation forest: worst on Berka, best at small n here
  • Crossover moves to n = 25–100 (stronger global model)
11 Variability sets the rate
Simulation. Regular people saturate by n = 50; travellers and variable people need about 500.
  • Under controlled manipulation: ρ = 0.48–0.71, all Holm p < 0.001
  • In real Berka accounts no descriptor predicts it (|ρ| ≤ 0.12)
12 Takeaways
Idea.A personal baseline ranks well from about 25 transactions.
Method.Provenance labels, paired windows, three data layers.
Result.A hybrid removes the cold-start penalty; calibration moves the operating point.
Practical.Shrink toward the crowd, keep recent history, calibrate out-of-sample.
